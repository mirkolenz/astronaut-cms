Every project we take on arrives with limits.
A deadline that will not move, a budget that is already spent, a platform we cannot change.
It is tempting to treat these as the enemy of good work.

We have come to see them as the opposite.

## Constraints make decisions for you

A blank canvas is paralysing because every option is still open.
The moment a real constraint appears, most of those options quietly disappear.
What remains is a much smaller space, and a much clearer problem.

That is a gift.
It means you can stop debating and start making.

## Fewer choices, better ones

When we design a system, we deliberately limit the palette before we begin.
Two typefaces, one accent colour, a single spacing scale.
The restriction is not aesthetic minimalism for its own sake.

It is that a small set of parts, used consistently, reads as intentional.
A large set, used loosely, reads as noise.

## The discipline of enough

The hardest part of the job is knowing when to stop.
Constraints help here too, because they define what enough looks like.

When the brief is honest about its limits, the finished product tends to feel calm.
Nothing is fighting for attention, because there was never room for it to.
